Own Your Space

Capture a space in 360 and 180 with a phone and a 3D-printed rig, stitch it yourself, and keep your own copy of every file. It's a low-cost way to document a place, and it sits alongside the professional scanning and GIS tools I use in my other work.

Rig360 and IR360

A 3D-printed, click-stop camera mount and a stitching app that turn a pocket thermal camera into a thermal 360. Each capture spot takes 36 stills across three tilt rings, and the stitched view keeps a temperature reading for every pixel.

One core, two swappable tops: a thermal top for a FLIR ONE Edge Pro and a phone top for regular 360s. Built during my Environmental Health & Safety internship at Montclair State. Now on version 1.1, with version 2 in the works.

Hawaiian Field Sites

A 360 field viewer built from ordinary phone photos on Maui and the Big Island in October 2026, with no 360 camera. Stand at any of 26 sites and look around.

Each site carries its location, compass direction, rock notes and sources. There is also a 3D map of both islands and a headset VR mode.

How I work

I came up through the trades as a machinist, welder and carpenter. Now I'm studying environmental science at Montclair State and pointing all of it at urban systems and sustainable infrastructure.

I believe in teaching and sharing knowledge freely. The quiz games and the Buzz Hub are open source, so anyone can use them, change them and pass them on.

Where it makes sense, I keep things simple and standalone. The quiz game shows need no accounts, so once you have them, you own them.

I use whatever fits the job, whether that's a welder, a GIS platform or AI. I set the constraints, check every result against the real world, and own what goes out under my name.
